1. What is Zelvero POS?

Zelvero POS is a point-of-sale and store-management system for a shop that sells products, takes cash or credit, and needs one place for stock, customers, suppliers, the cash drawer, and the day’s numbers.

It replaces the split between a notebook, a calculator, and a spreadsheet. A sale can reduce stock, update the drawer, and update a customer’s debt in the same step. The owner can see what sold, what came back, what is low, and who still owes money.

Why a business uses it
  • The cashier works from a product list with search and barcode, not from memory.
  • Prices can differ for retail, wholesale, and VIP customers.
  • USD and Lebanese pounds can be taken in the same sale, using the store’s exchange rate.
  • Credit sales and debt payments stay on the customer record.
  • Returns put stock back and adjust debt or the cash drawer.
  • Purchases can be unpaid, partly paid, or paid from the drawer, and can update product cost.
  • Profit is calculated from known cost. If cost was never entered, the system does not pretend the cost was zero.

Each store is separate. Staff see only the jobs the owner allows. The screen is available in Arabic and English, including right-to-left layout for Arabic.

2. Target customers

The product is a general retail and wholesale counter. It does not include restaurant tables, appointments, recipes, or pharmacy-specific rules. Do not advertise those.

A business that only needs a calculator and never tracks stock, debt, or cost will get less value. A business that already loses track of who owes money, what is on the shelf, or what the drawer should contain is the right audience.

4. Sales and POS workflow

  1. The cashier opens Point of sale. They need permission to sell.
  2. They search or scan. The product is added with a quantity. The cart shows the line price for the selected tier: retail, wholesale, or VIP.
  3. They can open another cart, up to ten, and name it. The first customer’s items stay put.
  4. They choose a currency for the cart and, if needed, a saved customer. Walk-in is allowed for a fully paid cash sale.
  5. Checkout offers full payment or credit. Credit requires a customer. The unpaid amount becomes debt.
  6. On a full payment they enter USD and LBP received. Change can be given in either currency. The drawer must be able to cover the change.
  7. If the customer has old debt and the connection is online, that old balance can be added to the amount collected. Old debt is paid before the new sale.
  8. Completing the sale records the invoice, reduces stock, and writes the drawer. A receipt can be printed.
  9. Later, from Sales, a permitted user opens the invoice and returns some or all quantities. Stock comes back. Debt or cash is adjusted. A return receipt can be printed.

If the connection drops, a completed sale can be saved on that device as pending. It is not an official invoice until the server accepts it. Stock, the official drawer, and the customer balance change at that moment, not when the offline proof is printed.

6. AI assistant

Do not advertise Zelvero AI to customers. The assistant is for internal Super Admin testing. Customer publication is blocked until a separate customer path is verified. It is not part of the offer a shop can buy today.

What the internal assistant is built to do, and only in that test environment:

  • Talk in Arabic and English about the test store.
  • Answer questions about sales, stock, low stock, and catalog items in that test store.
  • Open existing screens when asked, within the user’s permissions.
  • Prepare a product change for a person to confirm. It does not silently change the catalog from the model’s wording alone.
  • Accept voice on devices that support the in-browser speech tools, including an on-device transcription path. This is not a promise that every phone microphone works.

It is not a general business consultant, it does not see other shops’ data, and it is not a paid external AI service that the shop turns on. Future ideas are not features.

7. Security and user permissions

  • Sign-in is per person. The owner and each staff user are different accounts.
  • A staff user cannot open Users. Only the owner manages staff.
  • A staff user without a permission does not get that screen or that action. Examples: selling, seeing cost, opening the drawer, editing settings, importing products.
  • New staff start able to sell and view products, sales, customers, and reports. They do not start with product editing, imports, suppliers, the drawer, settings, costs, or sensitive data.
  • Each store’s data is separated from other stores. A user in one store does not browse another store’s sales.
  • Sensitive actions such as checkout, stock changes, returns, and debt payments are checked on the server, not only hidden in the menu.
  • Cost and profit can be hidden from staff who should not see buying prices.
  • Deleting a product that already has history is refused. Wiping a whole store requires the owner to type a confirmation.

Do not say the system is impossible to hack, unhackable, or 100 percent secure. Say that access is limited by account, role, and store.

9. Competitive advantages

Compared with a notebook: invoices, stock, debt, and the drawer stay tied together, and more than one person can work without sharing one notebook.

Compared with a spreadsheet: the cashier does not edit cells. A sale updates stock and the drawer. Permissions stop a cashier from seeing cost. The file is not the only copy sitting on one laptop, although a full one-click backup download is not offered.

Compared with a basic cash register: customer debt, supplier bills, two currencies in one sale, returns that restock, expiry, serial numbers, and profit that respects missing cost.

Compared with other POS products, only claim what Zelvero actually has: Arabic and English, USD and LBP together, credit customers, purchases on account, and role limits. Do not claim it is cheaper, faster, or more popular unless that is separately proven. Prices are not confirmed in this guide.

10. Real business scenarios

  • Morning check. The owner opens the dashboard and reads today’s net sales, returns, and debt collected. If they are allowed to see cost, they also see today’s gross profit.
  • Counter sale. The cashier scans a barcode, adds a second item, takes part of the money in USD and the rest in LBP, and prints the receipt.
  • Held order. A customer steps away. The cashier names that cart and opens a new one for the next person.
  • Credit. A known customer takes goods and pays part. The rest is added to their debt. Later they pay from the customer screen, and the drawer records the collection.
  • Low stock. The dashboard lists items under the minimum. The owner orders them and records a purchase, unpaid or partly paid.
  • Return. A customer brings two units back. Staff open the invoice, return those units, and the stock and the refund or debt reduction follow.
  • End of day. Someone with drawer permission counts USD and LBP. Any difference is stored. Expenses such as transport are entered in a category, not lost in a blank withdrawal.
  • New cashier. The owner creates a user who can sell and cannot see product cost or supplier bills.
  • Month review. Reports show net sales, then gross profit, expenses, and net profit for the chosen dates.

11. Marketing content reference

Strongest selling points

  • One counter for the sale, the stock, the drawer, and the customer’s debt.
  • USD and LBP in the same payment, with change in either currency.
  • Arabic and English.
  • Credit customers and supplier bills, including unpaid purchases.
  • Returns that fix stock and money.
  • Staff permissions, including hiding cost.
  • Profit that does not treat a missing cost as zero.

Problems and responses

What the shop saysAccurate response
“I already have a notebook.”The notebook does not update stock, the drawer, and debt together, and it cannot limit what a cashier sees.
“Customers pay in both currencies.”A sale can take USD and LBP, and change can be given in either, using the shop’s rate.
“People buy on account.”Credit sales and later payments stay on the customer, separate from new sales.
“I don’t trust profit numbers.”Gross profit uses known cost. Unknown cost is not turned into zero.
“What if the internet drops?”A sale can be saved on the device and sent later. Until then it is pending, not an official invoice.
“Can my cashier see what I pay for goods?”Only if you allow cost visibility. New staff do not get that by default.
“Do you have AI?”Not for customer stores. It is in internal testing and must not be offered.
“How much is it?”Not confirmed in this guide. Do not invent a price, plan, or trial length.

Reel and post ideas

  • Scan, mixed USD and LBP, receipt.
  • Switch the screen from English to Arabic.
  • Park one cart and serve the next customer.
  • A credit sale, then a debt payment on another day.
  • A return: two units back on the shelf.
  • Low-stock list, then a purchase that is not fully paid.
  • Count the drawer at closing.
  • Owner versus cashier: cost is hidden.
  • Today’s net sales versus today’s gross profit, with a one-line explanation.

Short descriptions

One line. Zelvero POS records the sale, the stock, the drawer, and what customers still owe — in Arabic or English, in USD and LBP.

Two lines. Built for shops that sell on the counter and on account. Staff see only what the owner allows, and profit is not faked when a cost was never entered.
